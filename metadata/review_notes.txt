PockeMoon is a nutrition journal for iPhone and iPad with a companion Apple Watch app. It targets iOS 17.0 and watchOS 10.0. The interface is in English; some dates and numbers use the en_US locale. The iOS app opens directly to Journal in a dark interface. There is no implemented onboarding, account, sign-in or dedicated demo mode.

Journal shows the selected day's energy, portion count, protein, fat and carbohydrates, an optional calorie-target progress ring, and entries grouped under Breakfast, Lunch, Dinner and Snack. Use the date picker to open past days or repeat a recent food. A new portion starts at 100 g, with a meal chosen from the current time and a date based on the selected day. The editor provides 50, 100, 150 and 250 g shortcuts, custom weights greater than zero and up to 10,000 g, meal selection and a date and time no later than the present. Save to log the portion; existing entries can be edited or deleted. Nutrients are scaled from values per 100 g. Missing values appear as a dash; daily totals sum known values and flag incomplete labels.

Add food opens a searchable catalogue containing 15 bundled Open Food Facts products and locally saved results. Filter the local list by name, brand or barcode, explicitly search Open Food Facts online, scan a barcode or enter a label manually. Manual entry accepts a name, brand, optional energy, protein, carbohydrate and fat values per 100 g, and ingredients. Selecting a product opens the portion editor and does not log it until saved. Rows show name, brand, energy per 100 g and a remote image when available. Product data can include ingredients, allergens and other nutrients, but there is no full product-detail or ingredient screen in the journal or portion editor.

Trends shows an ISO Monday-to-Sunday week, days logged, portion count, daily energy bars and a daily log. Navigate to earlier weeks and tap a non-future day to open its journal. Profile saves or clears an optional daily target from 1 to 20,000 kcal and links to Open Food Facts.

The watch shows today's energy, portion count, optional target progress and up to five recent distinct foods received from the phone. Selecting one asks for confirmation to log the same weight and meal today. The action is sent when the phone is reachable or queued for later transfer, with transfer status shown. A small or medium iOS widget shows today's energy, portion count and optional target progress; the medium widget also shows protein, fat and carbohydrates.

Journal entries and the target are stored as JSON in the app-group container shared with the widget. The catalogue caches up to 250 products in UserDefaults. WatchConnectivity transfers today's summary, target and recent portions to the watch and repeat actions back to the phone; the watch caches its last packet and pending actions in UserDefaults. Open Food Facts searches and barcode lookups use the network, and catalogue images may load from HTTPS URLs. The VisionKit scanner requests camera access when opened; users can type a barcode if scanning is unsupported or access is unavailable.

For review, create representative journal, trend and watch states through normal logging. Bundled foods include Quaker Oats and Nutella. There is no sample-journal switch; UI test source demonstrates logging bundled foods, editing a portion and setting a target.
